# Generative AI Applications: Research Report

## 1. Customer Support Chatbots

Generative AI chatbots can answer customer questions, explain products and services, and help users complete routine tasks. They can reduce support workload, provide faster responses, and make assistance available outside business hours.

Technical challenges include connecting the model to reliable company information, protecting personal data, and preventing incorrect or misleading answers. Existing implementations include [Intercom Fin](https://www.intercom.com/fin) — an AI customer service agent that answers questions using company knowledge; and [Zendesk AI](https://www.zendesk.com/ai/) — AI features that help support teams respond to and manage customer requests.

## 2. Code Generation and Developer Assistance

Generative AI tools can suggest code, explain unfamiliar code, generate tests, and help developers investigate errors. They may speed up routine programming tasks and help developers learn new technologies.

Challenges include incorrect or insecure code, outdated suggestions, and the need for developers to review generated output. Existing implementations include [GitHub Copilot](https://github.com/features/copilot) — an AI coding assistant that provides code suggestions and other development help; and [Amazon Q Developer](https://aws.amazon.com/q/developer/) — an AI assistant for software development and working with AWS.

## 3. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for Enterprise Knowledge

RAG applications combine a language model with a search system that retrieves relevant information from company documents. They can help employees find answers in internal policies, manuals, and knowledge bases using natural-language questions.

Technical challenges include preparing and indexing documents, retrieving the right passages, respecting access permissions, and ensuring answers are supported by sources. Existing implementations include [Azure AI Search](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/products/ai-services/ai-search) — a search service that can ground generative AI applications in organizational data; and [Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/knowledge-bases/) — a managed service for connecting foundation models to data sources through retrieval.

## 4. Marketing and Content Generation

Generative AI can help create or adapt marketing copy, product descriptions, campaign ideas, and other content. It can reduce the time needed for first drafts and help teams produce variations for different audiences.

Challenges include maintaining brand voice, checking factual claims, avoiding copyright or privacy problems, and reviewing content before publication. Existing implementations include [Jasper](https://www.jasper.ai/) — an AI content platform for marketing teams; and [Adobe Firefly](https://www.adobe.com/products/firefly.html) — a family of generative AI tools for creating and editing images and other creative assets.

## 5. Document Processing and Summarization

Generative AI can summarize long documents, extract key points, and turn unstructured text into structured drafts or summaries. This can help teams review information more quickly and reduce repetitive document work.

Challenges include missing important details, misinterpreting context, and handling confidential information safely. Human review is important when summaries influence business or legal decisions. Existing implementations include [Google Gemini for Workspace](https://workspace.google.com/solutions/ai/) — AI features integrated into Google Workspace apps; and [Microsoft 365 Copilot](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot) — an AI assistant that works across Microsoft 365 applications and content.
